Growth uses Cotality's 12-month median sale price for each council area (capitals: Greater Capital City area). Markets with fewer than 100 sales a year are left out. 2 · Rent growth vs price growth

The gap is rent growth minus price growth over the same period, in percentage points. A positive gap means rents rose faster than prices, so rental yields widened and the market got cheaper relative to its rent. That's the "prices stagnant while rents keep rising" pattern. A negative gap means prices outran rents. Each cell shows the gap, with the rent and price changes behind it underneath. Columns are the period: 1 year to 5 years. 3 · Days on market and stock on market

Days on market is the median number of days a property takes to sell over the last 12 months. Traffic Lights bands: ≤16 green (selling fast: the crowd is in), 17–44 orange, ≥45 red (slow). Stock on market is the share of all dwellings listed for sale over the last 12 months. Listings is the number of properties listed over the last 12 months. Falling days on market, stock and listings mean supply is drying up. Click a region to chart its history.

Source: Cotality Market Trends (12-month figures). Cotality changed how it measures listings, stock on market and days on market in July 2018, so earlier figures aren't comparable and the charts start in August 2018.

4 · Vacancy rate

Vacancy rate is the share of rental properties sitting empty, as a 3-month average to . Bands: below 2% tight (the Trading Strategy entry condition), 2–3% balanced, 3%+ loose. The 12-month average smooths out monthly noise, and is shown against the 12 months before. Low or falling vacancy usually comes before rent rises (see section 2 for rent vs price).
