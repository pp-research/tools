Key findings

1 · What our buying regions looked like on entry

  • Stagnant compared with other council areas. 3-year price growth was in the lowest 5–20% of all council areas (Melbourne's had fallen).
  • Listings already falling (−2% to −17%), with high rental yields in Townsville and Darwin (top 4–16%).
  • Still selling slowly: Townsville units 48 days on market, Darwin 57–60. Darwin had just started selling faster.
  • Rents had not yet surged (Darwin +2–3%). Confidence was low, which matches the "orange, not green" rule.
  • Since entry: Townsville , Darwin , Melbourne (Melbourne is still early).

2 · The two tests point to different regions

  • We bought into quiet markets. History rewards markets that have just started to turn: still stagnant, but sales volume is up, listings are down, homes sell faster and rents are rising. Those beat the typical market 69–71% of the time over 3 years.
  • Being stagnant on its own predicts nothing. "Stagnant but still weakening" is a trap: it beat the typical market only 33% of the time.
  • The belief that a market "underperformed its long-term average, so it will catch up" did not hold. Those markets kept lagging.

Method and caveats

  • Data: Cotality Market Trends council-area and capital-city files (monthly history 2000–2018 and monthly releases from Jul 2018 to Jun 2026); Cotality vacancy by council area (Apr 2023 – Jun 2026); building approvals for financial years 2024–26 from our 36-region file; Traffic Lights as published.
  • Ranking within each month: every condition is ranked against all council areas with 100+ sales in the trailing 12 months. This removes the national cycle and Cotality's methodology changes (vendor discount in Aug 2018, May 2020 and Nov 2024; the Jul 2018 changeover). Capital cities are ranked against council areas but are not included in them.
  • Entry dates were inferred from the data earlier in this project: Townsville Jul 2023, Darwin Aug 2024, Melbourne Jul 2025. Entry match is the average distance between ranks over 10 conditions, then expressed as the share of all council areas that are further away. Vacancy is left out because there is no data before 2023.
  • Forward growth: 1 year uses Cotality's automated valuation (AVM); 3 and 5 years use the 12-month median sale price, annualised.
  • Not tested historically: affordability, approvals (we only have 3 financial years), population and job ads. Traffic Lights covers these in the region detail but they are not part of either score.
  • Past patterns describe averages. A region can pass both tests and still underperform, which is why the flags matter.
