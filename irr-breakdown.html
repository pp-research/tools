How this is calculated

Each row is one calendar month, from the 1st of the purchase month to the 1st of the sale month. The IRR is XIRR over the Net CF column against those dates (actual/365, the Excel convention).

  • Interest = loan × (cash rate + margin + buffer) ÷ 12. Interest-only — no principal is amortised.
  • Property value compounds geometrically from purchase price to sold price across the hold period, and snaps to the sold price on the final row.
  • Running costs and rent are struck off that month's property value (rent uses the purchase/sold midpoint instead when the rent basis is Flat).
  • Net CF = holding cashflow, plus the deposit in month 1, plus (sold price − loan + selling costs) in the final month.
  • Negative gearing, when on, refunds the accumulated FY loss × marginal tax rate each June and at sale. The FY accumulator resets in July.
  • Hold period uses Excel's YEARFRAC basis 0 (US 30/360).

Columns that would hold the same value on every row are stated once above the table rather than repeated: the loan never changes, and neither does the cash rate on the Normalised basis. Accum loss (FY) and NG refund appear only when negative gearing is on, and Cash rate only on the Actual basis. Export CSV always writes every column, including the loan and cumulative interest, so it still ties column-for-column to the workbook.

Settlement-accurate timing — this is where the tool departs from the workbook. The sheet dates every cashflow to the 1st of its month and charges a full month of interest, rent and running costs at both ends of the hold. That books the sale up to 30 days early and charges roughly one month of costs too many. Here the deposit sits on the purchase date, the sale on the settlement date, and the first and last months accrue pro rata by day. Middle months are unaffected.

Across the 313 properties in the workbook it moves IRR by a median of 11 bps, but up to 167 bps — the effect is concentrated in short holds, where a part month is a larger slice of the term. Median shift is 55 bps under three years against 8 bps beyond six.

How the sold check works

Give it a list of sales and it matches them to the register by address, then tells you which of your properties the list says have sold — or, where a sale is already recorded, whether the two agree.

From RP Data

  1. Run a Recent Sale export for the suburbs or councils you want. Where to look on the Sold check tab is the list to work from: switch it to Suburb or Council, click one to copy it, paste it into RP Data's filter.
  2. Set the date range to cover the period you are checking. There is a export covering more than you need is fine — the check only ever looks at the properties on your register, so the rest of the file is ignored.
  3. Make sure the column set includes Street Address, Suburb, Sale Price and Sale Date. Keep Council Area in as well — it is how the council counts here fill in. Open in RPData gives each row a link back, which gets saved onto the property.
  4. Export as CSV or Excel and drop the file on this page.

From Pricefinder

  1. Export a sales result for the suburbs you want, as CSV or Excel.
  2. It needs an address, a sale price and a sale date. Pricefinder calls the suburb Locality and the buyer Purchaser — the tool reads either name, and uses those two to recognise the file as Pricefinder's and tag the sales accordingly.
  3. Drop the file on this page. If it names no council the Council list stays uncounted; everything else works the same.

From any other sheet

Anything with an address, a sale price and a sale date in columns named so. The headings do not have to be on the first row — a title and the filters the report was run with are fine above them. If a column is named something it does not recognise, Columns used lets you point it at the right one.

Columns named Owner, Vendor, Purchaser or Agent are names of people and are never read. The window lists them back so you can see they were skipped.

Once it is loaded

  1. Choose what to check. Not sold yet asks "has this sold?" of the properties with no sale recorded. Every property turns the same export into a check on the sale prices already in the book. With almost nothing waiting on a sale it opens on Every property and says so.
  2. Read what it found.
    • Sold the address matched exactly, down to the unit and the street type
    • Check the number, street and suburb match but the unit or the street type does not — worth your eyes
    • Differs it is already sold and the export disagrees, with a chip saying whether it was the price, the date or both. Your figure and theirs sit side by side
    • Agrees the book and the export say the same thing
    • No sale found the suburb is in the export but this address is not
    • Suburb not in export the export does not cover it at all — the badge beside Where to look counts how many properties that is
  3. Write it on, row by row. Mark sold records the sale price, the date, the link and the source. On a row that disagrees the button says Use the export instead, because it replaces a figure rather than filling a blank. Mark all sold only ever touches rows with no sale yet that matched exactly — a disagreement is never written in bulk.
Nothing is written until you press one of those buttons, and everything written goes into History with your name against it. One to watch: where RP Data gives a settlement date and you recorded a contract date, that shows as Differs — date. That is a real difference worth seeing, not an error, so do not accept those in bulk.

What this reads

A list of properties as they were bought, or a list of sales. Which one is worked out from the columns the file names — a sales export names a sale price and a sale date and nothing about what the place cost to buy. The preview says which it read it as before anything is written.

Headed columns are read by name — Property or Address, Suburb, State, Postcode, Purchase Price, Purchase Date, Market, Notes — in any order. Without a header the order above is assumed; a leading row-number column is skipped. A single column of addresses is read as properties with nothing known about them yet. Dates may be Excel serials (42565), 14/07/2016, 2016-07-14 or 14 Jul 2016. Owner, Vendor, Purchaser and Agent columns are names of people and are never read.

Recording these sales

Recorded as entered by hand. A sale checked against RP Data or Pricefinder is marked as such on the Sold check tab, which is where that check happens.
