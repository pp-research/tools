How this is built, and what to watch for

Listings. Cotality Market Trends, one workbook per month, Aug 2018 to Jun 2026. Sale listings before that come from Cotality’s older monthly history files (see Earlier years). Both lines are rolling 12-month totals: sale listings use “# of listings last 12 months” and rental listings use “# of rental listings in last 12 months”. Cotality does not publish a 1-month rental count, so there is no like-for-like monthly view. Capitals use Cotality’s capital-city rows (Greater Capital City areas). The 28 regional markets use their council (LGA) rows, the same split the Traffic Lights tool uses.

Monthly view (estimated). Cotality publishes rental listings only as a 12-month total, and its 1-month sale count is not a monthly share of the 12-month count: it counts every property advertised during the month, including those still on the market from earlier months, so it runs at about 27% of the 12-month figure rather than 1/12. To put both lines on the same basis, the monthly view spreads each 12-month total across its months: month estimate = 12-month total ÷ 12 × (this month’s pattern ÷ the pattern’s average over the same 12 months). Sale listings take their pattern from Cotality’s 1-month sale count; rental listings take theirs from Cotality’s 1-month vacancy rate. Over a year the estimates add back to roughly the real 12-month total (usually within about 10% for sale and 15% for rental, more in small markets). Cotality’s own 1-month sale count is shown in the hover box as “Sale listings advertised in month”. The rental gap (Dec 2023 – Oct 2024) stays empty because there is no 12-month total to spread.

Population. ABS Regional Population 2024–25 (3218.0): Greater Capital City areas for the eight capitals and LGAs for the rest. Each 30 June figure matches the Traffic Lights tool exactly. Months between two June figures are interpolated in a straight line. Months after Jun 2025 (all of 2026 so far) use the Jun 2025 figure, the latest ABS estimate, until the ABS publishes 2025–26.

Rental gap. Cotality’s files carry no rental listings from Dec 2023 to Oct 2024, in every market. The gap is left empty, not filled. Treat any level change across it with caution.

Count-method changes. A 12-month total can’t move 15% or more in a single month from real activity, so a jump that size is marked as a change in how Cotality counts. The big ones are Mar 2020 (capitals), May 2020 (rentals down about 23% everywhere) and Jan 2021 (rentals back up about 33%). Nov 2024 and Jan 2026 move listings between houses and units, which is why the All view is steadier than Houses or Units alone. Compare levels within a stretch between markers, not across one.

Earlier years. Jan 2001 to Jun 2018 come from Cotality’s older history files: the capital-city file and the ten LGA files. The old files count more listings than the monthly files at the same date, by different amounts in each market. For houses the old sale count runs from 5% lower (Newcastle) to 68% higher (Perth). So each market’s houses and units, sale and rental, are rescaled separately, so that Jun 2018 meets the Aug 2018 level, and that scale is held for every earlier month. Both lines are drawn lighter before Aug 2018. Switch Before Aug 2018 to As published to see the older files’ own figures without rescaling; the lines then step at Aug 2018.

Early rental listings are understated. Cotality’s rental coverage kept growing until about 2016. Capitals’ rental listings went from 1.5% of population in 2008 to 3.7% in 2016 before levelling off, which is far more than the rental market itself moved. Some regional markets barely register before 2010 (Launceston houses: 7 rental listings a year in 2008). Treat the rental line before about 2016 as showing direction only, not level. Sale listing coverage settled around 2008: regional sale listings more than doubled from 2004 to 2008, then flattened. The chart can go back to Jan 2001 at most, because that is where the ABS population estimates start (30 June 2001, held for Jan–May 2001). Before about 2006 many markets have no rental count, and some none for sale (Launceston has no sale count until Feb 2008). The All 36 markets line therefore adds up only the markets reporting each month and divides by those markets’ population, so a missing market drops out of both sides. The mix of markets changes as each one starts reporting; the hover note gives the count for each month.

Jul 2018. The month Cotality switched council boundaries. Merged councils are missing (Central Coast shows 423 sale listings), and the older files end in Jun 2018, so the month is left empty.

Rate overlay. Both rates come from the RBA. Cash rate is the cash rate target at month end, drawn as steps (table F1; before 2011 the RBA file holds monthly averages, so a month with a change takes the new rate). Bank Rate (from mid-2004, when the RBA series starts) is the banks’ discounted variable rate for owner-occupier housing loans (table F5, indicator lending rates). It sits close to what new borrowers actually pay; the standard variable rate runs about 2 points higher. Both read off the right-hand % axis.
