How this is built, and what to watch for

Listings. Cotality Market Trends, one workbook per month, Aug 2018 to Jun 2026. Sale listings before that come from Cotality’s older monthly history files (see Earlier years). Both lines are rolling 12-month totals: sale listings use “# of listings last 12 months” and rental listings use “# of rental listings in last 12 months”. Cotality does not publish a 1-month rental count, so there is no like-for-like monthly view. Capitals use Cotality’s capital-city rows (Greater Capital City areas). The 28 regional markets use their council (LGA) rows, the same split the Traffic Lights tool uses.

Population. ABS Regional Population 2024–25 (3218.0): Greater Capital City areas for the eight capitals and LGAs for the rest. Each 30 June figure matches the Traffic Lights tool exactly. Months between two June figures are interpolated in a straight line. Months after Jun 2025 (all of 2026 so far) use the Jun 2025 figure, the latest ABS estimate, until the ABS publishes 2025–26.

Rental gap. Cotality’s files carry no rental listings from Dec 2023 to Oct 2024, in every market. The gap is left empty, not filled. Treat any level change across it with caution.

Count-method changes. A 12-month total can’t move 15% or more in a single month from real activity, so a jump that size is marked as a change in how Cotality counts. The big ones are Mar 2020 (capitals), May 2020 (rentals down about 23% everywhere) and Jan 2021 (rentals back up about 33%). Nov 2024 and Jan 2026 move listings between houses and units, which is why the All view is steadier than Houses or Units alone. Compare levels within a stretch between markers, not across one.

Earlier years. Jan 2008 to Jun 2018 come from Cotality’s older history files: the capital-city file and the ten LGA files. The old files count more listings than the monthly files at the same date, by different amounts in each market. For houses the old sale count runs from 5% lower (Newcastle) to 68% higher (Perth). So each market’s houses and units, sale and rental, are rescaled separately, so that Jun 2018 meets the Aug 2018 level, and that scale is held for every earlier month. Both lines are drawn lighter before Aug 2018 to show they have been rescaled.

Early rental listings are understated. Cotality’s rental coverage kept growing until about 2016. Capitals’ rental listings went from 1.5% of population in 2008 to 3.7% in 2016 before levelling off, which is far more than the rental market itself moved. Some regional markets barely register before 2010 (Launceston houses: 7 rental listings a year in 2008). Treat the rental line before about 2016 as showing direction only, not level. Sale listings start in 2008 because their coverage settled by then: regional sale listings more than doubled from 2004 to 2008, then flattened.

Jul 2018. The month Cotality switched council boundaries. Merged councils are missing (Central Coast shows 423 sale listings), and the older files end in Jun 2018, so the month is left empty.

Rate overlay. Both rates come from the RBA. Cash rate is the cash rate target at month end, drawn as steps (table F1; before 2011 the RBA file holds monthly averages, so a month with a change takes the new rate). Bank Rate is the banks’ discounted variable rate for owner-occupier housing loans (table F5, indicator lending rates). It sits close to what new borrowers actually pay; the standard variable rate runs about 2 points higher. Both read off the right-hand % axis.
